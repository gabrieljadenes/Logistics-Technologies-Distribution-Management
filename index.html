Tutorial Module 01 • Study Guide

Total Logistics Cost (TLC) & Multimodal Shipping

Understanding how freight carriers price cargo using Actual Weight vs. Volumetric Weight, Revenue Tons, and multimodal leg combinations.

1. Why Do Carriers Use Volumetric (Dimensional) Weight?

Aircraft, trucks, and ocean containers are constrained by both weight capacity and cubic volume capacity. Light, bulky cargo ("balloon freight") occupies valuable space before reaching weight limits. Therefore, carriers charge based on whichever is greater: Actual Gross Weight (AW) or Volumetric Weight (VW).

Air Freight Standard
DIM Factor = 6,000

$\text{VW (kg)} = \frac{L \times W \times H \text{ (cm)}}{6,000} \times \text{Qty}$
(Equivalent to $1\text{ CBM} = 166.67\text{ kg}$)

Road / Express Standard
DIM Factor = 5,000

$\text{VW (kg)} = \frac{L \times W \times H \text{ (cm)}}{5,000} \times \text{Qty}$
(Equivalent to $1\text{ CBM} = 200\text{ kg}$)

Sea Freight LCL (W/M)
Revenue Ton (RT)

$\text{RT} = \max\left(\text{Metric Tons}, \text{CBM}\right)$ where $1\text{ MT} = 1,000\text{ kg}$ and $1\text{ CBM} = 10^6\text{ cm}^3$.

2. Step-by-Step Tutorial Cases

Worked Example: Air Consignment

10 cartons, each $15\text{ kg}$ and $50 \times 40 \times 30\text{ cm}$. Air rate: $\$5.00/\text{kg}$ (DIM 6000).

• Total AW = 10 × 15 kg = 150 kg
• Total VW = [10 × (50 × 40 × 30)] / 6,000 = 600,000 / 6,000 = 100 kg
• Chargeable Weight (CW) = max(150, 100) = 150 kg → Total Cost = 150 × $5.00 = $750.00
Question 2: Multimodal Journey (Road + Air)

20 boxes, $40\text{ kg}$ each, $60 \times 50 \times 50\text{ cm}$. Leg 1 Road (Shenzhen → HKG): DIM 5000, $\$0.50/\text{kg}$. Leg 2 Air (HKG → Frankfurt): DIM 6000, $\$3.20/\text{kg}$ base + $\$0.30/\text{kg}$ fuel surcharge.

• Total AW = 800 kg | Total Volume = 3,000,000 cm³ (3.00 CBM)
• Leg 1 (Road): VW = 3,000,000 / 5,000 = 600 kg → CW₁ = max(800, 600) = 800 kg → Cost₁ = 800 × $0.50 = $400
• Leg 2 (Air): VW = 3,000,000 / 6,000 = 500 kg → CW₂ = max(800, 500) = 800 kg → Cost₂ = 800 × ($3.20 + $0.30) = $2,800
• Total Multimodal Cost = $400 + $2,800 = $3,200.00

Tutorial Module 02 • Study Guide

North-West Corner Method (NWCM)

Generating an initial basic feasible solution for multi-warehouse distribution networks.

1. Purpose & Core Mechanics

The North-West Corner Method (NWCM) is a fast heuristic used in transportation problems to allocate supply from factory warehouses ($F_1 \dots F_m$) to regional demand centers ($C_1 \dots C_n$).

Strengths

Extremely simple and fast to compute by hand or code; guarantees an initial feasible solution that satisfies all supply and demand constraints without loops.

Critical Limitation

NWCM is purely positional—it completely ignores unit transportation costs ($C_{ij}$) during allocation. Therefore, the resulting total cost is rarely optimal and requires further optimization (e.g., Stepping Stone, MODI, or Linear Programming).

2. Worked Example & Student Scenarios (From Lecture Slides 58–62)

Illustrative Example (3×3 Matrix)

Supply: $F_1=30, F_2=40, F_3=10$ (Total = 80). Demand: $C_1=20, C_2=30, C_3=30$ (Total = 80). Unit costs: Row 1 $[4, 8, 5]$, Row 2 $[7, 3, 9]$, Row 3 $[6, 4, 7]$.

1. F1 → C1: min(30, 20) = 20 units @ $4 = $80 (C1 satisfied, F1 rem = 10)
2. F1 → C2: min(10, 30) = 10 units @ $8 = $80 (F1 exhausted, C2 rem = 20)
3. F2 → C2: min(40, 20) = 20 units @ $3 = $60 (C2 satisfied, F2 rem = 20)
4. F2 → C3: min(20, 30) = 20 units @ $9 = $180 (F2 exhausted, C3 rem = 10)
5. F3 → C3: min(10, 10) = 10 units @ $7 = $70 (All satisfied!)
Total Transportation Cost = $80 + $80 + $60 + $180 + $70 = $470

Tutorial Module 03 • Study Guide

Inventory Optimization: EOQ vs. Staggered EPQ

Complete theoretical breakdown and worked solutions from Tutorial #7.

1. The Optimization Principle (Basic EOQ)

Total annual inventory cost consists of Annual Ordering Cost $\left(\frac{D}{Q}S\right)$, which decreases as order size $Q$ grows, and Annual Holding Cost $\left(\frac{Q}{2}H\right)$, which increases linearly with $Q$. Total cost is minimized at the exact point where Ordering Cost equals Holding Cost:

(D / Q) × S = (Q / 2) × H ⇒ Q² = 2DS / H ⇒ Q* = √(2DS / H)

2. Non-Instantaneous Replenishment (EPQ in Distribution Logistics)

Basic EOQ assumes the entire batch $Q^*$ hits the receiving dock all at once (instantaneous receipt). In real distribution centers, goods often arrive via staggered daily truck shuttles or continuous kitting lines at an inbound rate $p$ while customer orders are simultaneously dispatched at daily rate $d$ (where $p > d$):

Phase 1: Inbound & Dispatch Window

Goods arrive at rate $p$ and leave at rate $d$. Stock accumulates at a slower net rate of $(p - d)$. Peak warehouse inventory $I_{\max} = Q_p^*\left(1 - \frac{d}{p}\right)$ never reaches full batch size $Q_p^*$.

Phase 2: Pure Dispatch

Inbound shuttle stops once $Q_p^*$ units have been delivered. Outbound dispatch continues at daily rate $d$ until warehouse stock hits zero.
